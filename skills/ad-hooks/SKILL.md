# Ad hooks — the first two seconds, by mechanic

A hook is a VISUAL device, not a clever line. The 17 mechanics below are the ones that keep showing up in the
longest-running ads across Meta, TikTok and YouTube. Use them by name when you brief or write an ad.

## The mechanics
1. **Direct callout** — the creator leans into the lens and names the exact viewer in the first second ("if you can't fall asleep, watch this").
2. **Open mid-problem** — drop straight into the painful, relatable moment before any setup; the pain is the hook.
3. **Before → after snap** — an instant cut from the bad state to the good state inside ~2 seconds.
4. **Object into frame** — the product slides, rolls or is set down INTO frame with a confident thunk.
5. **Big reaction** — a genuine gasp, jaw-drop or "wait, WHAT" opens the clip; the emotion stops the thumb.
6. **Satisfying macro** — an oddly-satisfying extreme close-up: a pour, a peel, a snap, a texture.
7. **Pattern interrupt** — one unexpected, slightly absurd visual in the very first frame.
8. **Provocative question** — a bold, specific on-screen question or claim the target cannot scroll past.
9. **Shock stat** — a surprising number fills the screen as the cold open.
10. **POV** — first person; the viewer IS the character ("POV: you finally…").
11. **Whip / snap-zoom** — a fast whip-pan or snap-zoom that rushes into the subject.
12. **Unexpected place** — the product somewhere surprising, played completely straight.
13. **Countdown to reveal** — a quick 3-2-1 build that pays off on an immediate reveal.
14. **List tease** — promise a numbered payoff ("3 reasons I'll never go back to…") to open a curiosity loop.
15. **Mishap → deadpan** — a soft mishap in the first beat, then the creator carries on unbothered.
16. **Street interview** — a handheld-mic vox-pop; a stranger's hot take, then the product, then the reaction.
17. **Surreal-calm setting** — the creator calmly reviews the product somewhere impossible (a volcano rim, a skyscraper edge).

## Picking one
- Talking-head / UGC: 1, 2, 10, 15, 16 fit naturally; 6 and 4 work when the product has a satisfying physical action.
- Product-only (no person on screen): 4, 6, 12, 13, 3 — never 1 or 10, which need a face.
- On-screen text hooks (8, 9, 14) only when the format will actually render text cleanly; on a plain video model, text
  garbles — composite it in post or skip these.
- Apps and software: 3 (before/after of the screen), 8, 9, 14; show the real interface inside 3 seconds.
- Regulated or serious categories (health, finance): avoid 15 and 17; prefer 2, 8, 9 with a specific, true claim.

## Writing the beat
Write the hook as ONE sentence of action a camera can film, then the line spoken or shown, then the cut. Example:
"Object into frame: a hand sets the bottle down on the marble with a thunk, label to camera. Line: 'Ninety minutes of hot
yoga and it did not move.' Cut to the product in use." Then check: is the product on screen by second three?

## Testing
Make three versions of the same ad that differ ONLY in the hook mechanic, run them against each other, and keep the
mechanic that wins — the body of the ad is rarely the reason one loses.
